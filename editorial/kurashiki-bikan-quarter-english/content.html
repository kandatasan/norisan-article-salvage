My wife made a fair point one day: “We always go north or west, don't we?” She was right. From Hiroshima, our weekends usually involve Yamaguchi or the San'in coast. So we decided to do something different and head east, into Okayama.

Our destination was Kurashiki Bikan Historical Quarter, famous for its white-walled buildings, little shops and canal-like river. We had never wandered the old district together before. I expected a pleasant stroll. What I didn't expect was to spend part of it shouting about a fish while my wife walked away.

It turned into a very good day. There was an extravagant fruit parfait, an even better citrus parfait than I'd expected, a river full of distractions and denim shops that seriously tested my self-control.

This is the order we actually enjoyed Kurashiki, with a few tips for visiting from Hiroshima, Okayama or farther afield.

Travel note: Personal experiences and photographs are from our trip in warm summer weather. Café menus and shop hours change; location and access details were checked against official tourism and business information in November 2026.

Start with fruit parfaits at Kurashiki Momoko

Before we even reached the historic streets, we went for parfaits at Kurashiki Momoko. If we're visiting Okayama—the prefecture famous for its fruit—it seems only right to start with something fruity.

My wife chose a peach and Shine Muscat grape parfait. I remember looking at the price and feeling a little intimidated. In my memory it was close to ¥4,000, but that's what I remember from our visit, not a current menu price.

So I chose a less expensive citrus parfait. A practical financial decision, obviously.

Then I tasted it. The balance of sweet, tart and just a hint of bitterness was fantastic. I kept scraping the edge of the glass with my spoon to get the last bit. No regrets about that choice.

There are several Kurashiki Momoko locations and menus differ by branch and season. If you want a particular parfait, check the official shop and opening information rather than assuming our old order is still available.

Walk along the Kurashiki River and its white-walled streets

With dessert taken care of, we finally entered the Bikan Historical Quarter. The mood changes quickly: old white-walled warehouses, willow trees, small bridges, shops tucked into traditional buildings and a river down the middle.

You don't really need an elaborate sightseeing plan to enjoy this part of Kurashiki. We simply walked along the water and looked into whatever caught our attention. It's one of those neighborhoods where doing very little feels like doing exactly the right thing.

One of my favorite things about the scenery was how the bridges made the view change. Step onto a bridge, look back along the river, and the white buildings line up in a completely new way.

Then I spotted a fish. My wife kept walking.

While we were looking at the river, something moved in the water. A fish. Not just any fish: I thought I recognized a snakehead.

“Snakehead! There's a snakehead swimming there!”

I got far more excited than the average tourist probably would. When I turned to share the discovery with my wife, she was already a long way down the street.

There I was, apparently shouting about fish at the picturesque river while everyone else admired the architecture. A very normal husband-and-wife sightseeing moment.

There was even a swan on the water. Quite beautiful. But at that moment, I was still thinking about the fish.

I'm a fishing enthusiast, so little surprises like that are part of what makes a walk memorable for me. You may be more interested in the boats, buildings and swans. All perfectly reasonable choices.

The denim shops were dangerous — for my wallet

Kurashiki's shopping streets are also a good place to browse for denim and other local goods. We stepped into a denim shop, and I kept finding things I liked. It wasn't just the jeans; the small accessories and designs were dangerously tempting.

The only reason I managed not to buy something might have been the heat. It was one of those ferociously hot summer days when your brain keeps reminding you that you are hot, even while you're trying to admire a nice piece of clothing.

If we return on a cooler autumn day, my wallet may not be as fortunate.

Is Kurashiki Bikan Historical Quarter easy to visit without a car?

Yes. This is one of the easiest places on our travel blog to recommend to visitors using public transport. The official Okayama tourism guide puts the historic quarter at around a 15-minute walk from JR Kurashiki Station.

We came on a wider drive, but you don't need to copy our transport choice just to explore the old district. If you're based in Okayama or Hiroshima and prefer trains, check the current rail connections and enjoy the walking area at your own pace.

See the Okayama official visitor guide for the location, access information and municipal parking details.

How much time should you allow?

We didn't try to check off every famous museum or attraction. We came for a relaxed walk, dessert and a little shopping. For that style of visit, allow a few hours and make room for unplanned stops.

If you want to enter the Ohara Museum of Art, ride a traditional riverboat or visit other landmarks, allow extra time and check their separate ticket arrangements. We didn't do those things on this visit, so I'm not going to pretend to review them.

A few tips before you visit

  • Start early on hot days: the historic streets are pleasant for walking, but summer heat can be exhausting.
  • Check your parfait shop: Kurashiki Momoko has multiple branches and seasonal menus.
  • Wear comfortable shoes: much of the appeal is simply wandering through the old neighborhood.
  • Leave shopping time: the small denim and craft shops are worth a look even if you don't plan to buy anything.
  • Don't overplan: we had fun following the little things we noticed rather than racing from sight to sight.

Would I go back?

Yes. We'd spent so many holidays driving toward Yamaguchi or the San'in coast that heading east felt like a change in itself. But the real reward was how easy Kurashiki made it to enjoy a simple day: excellent fruit, pretty streets, a few shops and one extremely distracting fish.

Next time I'd choose a cooler season. We'd probably wander a little longer. And I may need to keep a closer eye on both my wallet and my wife when we go past the denim shops.
