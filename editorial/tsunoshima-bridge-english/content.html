The first time I saw Tsunoshima Bridge, I couldn't help saying, “Wow.” I've been to the area more than once, and that view still gets me: a long road stretching across an almost impossibly blue sea. It looks like something from a television commercial. From the right spot, it's even better.

My wife and I visited Tsunoshima Island in Yamaguchi Prefecture during our September 2023 road trip from Hiroshima. We didn’t just photograph the bridge and leave. We crossed it, climbed Tsunoshima Lighthouse, stopped for a drink and bought souvenirs. I'm glad we made time for the island itself.

This guide follows what we actually did, with the photo viewpoints and practical details I would want to know before a first visit.

Travel note: All personal impressions and photos are from September 2023. Bridge rules and lighthouse visitor information were checked with official sources in November 2026. Weather, fees and opening arrangements may change.

Is Tsunoshima Bridge worth the drive?

Absolutely, if you enjoy coastal scenery and road trips. The 1,780-metre Tsunoshima Ohashi Bridge connects mainland Yamaguchi to Tsunoshima Island, and crossing it is free. Even before driving over, the bridge and the sea make a beautiful scene together.

Here's my advice, though: don't treat the bridge as your entire itinerary. Allow around two to three hours to see the viewpoints, explore the lighthouse area, stop for a drink and browse for souvenirs. That was a more rewarding visit for us than simply taking a picture from the roadside.

Three places I liked for bridge photos

1. Amagase Park — the classic wide view

We started around Amagase Park, on the mainland side. From here you can take in the whole scene: turquoise water, the island in the distance and the pale road reaching toward it.

This is where I'd send a first-time visitor with only a few minutes for photographs. The official Yamaguchi tourism site lists free parking at the park, so you don't need to stop illegally along the road to enjoy the view.

2. The bridge monument area

The monument around the bridge entrance makes a nice souvenir-photo stop. It has that unmistakable “we made it to Tsunoshima!” feeling, with the bridge behind you.

3. A slightly higher view over the road

A little higher up, the perspective changes. The road seems to disappear into the sea, and you get a much better sense of the bridge's length. I liked comparing the photos from the different angles instead of taking ten versions of the same shot.

One important rule: do not stop or park on the bridge itself to take pictures. Use a designated viewpoint or car park.

Cross the bridge — then keep going to Tsunoshima Lighthouse

After the famous bridge, we drove farther into the island to Tsunoshima Lighthouse. I’m glad we did. The old stone lighthouse looks beautiful against the sky, and going inside gives the trip another little adventure.

The lighthouse first lit in 1876 and is still operating. Its spiral staircase has 105 steps. They're narrow enough that you know you're climbing a real lighthouse, not riding an elevator to an observation deck.

If steep steps are difficult for you, think about that before buying a ticket. I enjoyed the climb, but it is not an accessible lift ride.

The official lighthouse operator currently lists a ¥300 visitor contribution for junior-high-school age and older. Published opening hours are 9:00–17:00 from March to September and 9:00–16:30 from October to February, with final entry 20 minutes before closing. Check the official Tsunoshima Lighthouse page for the latest details.

The little lighthouse museum is worth a look

Beside the lighthouse, we also looked around the museum. You get a glimpse of the people and technology behind this landmark. It's a quieter stop than the photo-famous bridge, but it makes the visit feel less like a drive-by.

A bubbly drink and souvenir hunting

We stopped at Granvista Tsunoshima near the lighthouse area. I ordered a drink called Shuwa Pop. Its fizzy, crackling ice was wonderfully silly in the best way. After climbing the lighthouse and wandering outside, that cold drink hit the spot.

We also visited Tsunoshima Sohonke to look for local souvenirs. I bought a sea-urchin product there. It was an extra piece of the island to take home, beyond the bridge photographs.

For current opening information, check Granvista Tsunoshima and Tsunoshima Sohonke. The menu or products you find may differ from ours.

How to get to Tsunoshima Bridge

Tsunoshima is in the northwest of Yamaguchi Prefecture, far enough from Hiroshima that I would not combine it with a string of distant stops on a rushed day trip. On our actual journey, we stayed the previous night in Nagato before visiting the island.

You can reach the bridge area by public transport, but connections are limited and seeing the lighthouse and shops takes additional planning. For our sightseeing route, having a car made the day much simpler.

Weather and driving precautions

  • Strong winds can close Tsunoshima Bridge. Check the weather and Yamaguchi road information before a stormy visit.
  • Amagase Park is a good place to photograph the bridge safely; the road and bridge are not parking spaces.
  • Sunny weather makes the blue sea especially vivid, but take your own photos rather than trying to reproduce someone else's exact angle.
  • Holiday traffic can be slow. An early start gives you more room to explore the island.

My verdict: the island deserves more than one photograph

The bridge was why I wanted to come. That view alone still makes me grin. But looking back, climbing the lighthouse, laughing over the Shuwa Pop and choosing a souvenir are what made the stop feel like a proper holiday.

If you're already driving around western Yamaguchi, Tsunoshima makes a lovely companion to Motonosumi Shrine. We visited both on our wider two-day journey, but do check current access restrictions at the shrine before following that itinerary.
