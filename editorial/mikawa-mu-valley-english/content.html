Would you explore an underground kingdom inside an old mine, armed with a map, a flashlight and a book of puzzles?

My wife and I did exactly that at Mikawa Mu Valley in Iwakuni, Yamaguchi Prefecture. No children along. Just two adults who were probably far too excited to pretend otherwise.

We visited in September 2023 on the way from Hiroshima toward Nagato. What started as an underground adventure turned into a surprisingly serious puzzle-solving challenge. Then we tried gold panning, and I found real gold on my first scoop.

Was it scary? A little eerie, perhaps. But it was a lot more fun than frightening.

About this review: Our experiences and original photos are from September 2023. The puzzle program has changed since then. Prices, entry hours and attractions mentioned as current were checked on official pages in November 2026; please confirm again before visiting.

Is Mikawa Mu Valley scary for adults?

Parts of the mine are dark, and the ancient-looking statues and passages are deliberately mysterious. If you're afraid of dark or enclosed spaces, some sections may be uncomfortable.

But for us, the curiosity won. It felt much more like stepping into an RPG than visiting a haunted house.

The setting is an old mine transformed into a fantasy underground kingdom. The supposed lost ancient civilization is part of the attraction's fictional story, not a real archaeological discovery.

What we did—and how long it took

We spent almost three hours there despite being in a hurry to continue our Yamaguchi road trip.

Our rough breakdown was about two hours exploring the mine and solving the puzzles, half an hour gold panning, and half an hour for lunch. If I planned the day again, I'd allow four or five hours so we wouldn't have to rush.

The underground route extends for roughly one kilometer. Current puzzle-course durations vary, so don't confuse the time printed for the puzzle activity with the time needed for your whole visit.

The puzzle challenge: we chose the difficult setting

At the time we visited, there were different difficulty options. We chose the hard one.

I wasn't worried about getting stuck. If the puzzles turned out to be too difficult, surely we could just look up the answer on a smartphone.

A rather clever idea, I thought.

Then we lost our phone signal

We reached a question where I wasn't completely sure of the answer. Out came the phone.

No signal.

Of course. We were underground.

My cunning plan lasted about as long as you'd expect. From then on, it was just the two of us trying to solve everything ourselves.

And honestly, that made the experience better. Looking for clues, thinking together and reaching the end without checking answers felt genuinely rewarding.

I'm not publishing the puzzle answers. That would spoil the whole point.

Hidden rooms, mysterious objects and underground water

The puzzles aren't the only interesting part. The mine contains unusual decorations, old-civilization-themed displays and areas that can be easy to walk past if you're focused only on the next challenge.

One room even contained a gold-colored jet-like object. Why would an ancient underground kingdom have a golden aircraft?

I decided it was best not to worry about that.

There were also illuminated water features deeper inside, and the sound of flowing water echoing through the rock.

These are the sorts of things that make the cave feel like a game world rather than just an old tunnel.

What to wear: the cave was 18°C in September

It was still hot outside during our late-September visit. But the temperature displayed at the underground entrance was 18°C.

It felt like natural air conditioning. I'd recommend comfortable clothes, a removable light layer, and sneakers with good grip. There are slopes and stairs, and the ground isn't where I'd want to wear slippery sandals.

If you're also planning to try outdoor gold panning, consider bringing sandals and a small towel for that separate activity.

Gold panning: the first scoop was a winner

After leaving the underground section, I noticed the kingdom's little shrine and wished for good fortune.

Then we headed to the gold-panning attraction.

We listened to the staff's explanation and started sorting through the sand with a pan.

And there it was. Actual gold on the first scoop.

I felt unstoppable. Surely there would be more.

There wasn't.

I kept trying, but the next little piece came only right near the end of the session—and it was even smaller.

Apparently getting rich as a gold prospector isn't so easy.

The outdoor pond was fun as well. I waded in barefoot, enjoying the cool water.

Gemstone hunting is another attraction, but we didn't try it. Our choice was gold, thanks to my brief moment of overconfidence.

Lunch: pyramid curry and a strangely named udon

After the cave and the gold, it was time to eat.

We tried pyramid curry and a themed udon dish. The curry was pleasantly familiar, and the udon name made me laugh before I'd even started eating.

Those were the dishes we ate in 2023, not a guarantee of today's menu. The restaurant doesn't necessarily open every day the park does. Check the official operating calendar.

Admission, opening hours and getting there

According to the official visitor information checked in November 2026, standard admission is ¥2,600 for adults and junior-high-school students, ¥1,200 for elementary-school children, and free for preschoolers. Gold panning and gemstone hunting are optional paid experiences, listed at ¥1,650 and ¥990 respectively. Parking is free.

General opening hours are currently 10:00 a.m.–5:00 p.m., with a 4:00 p.m. final admission for the regular underground experience. Some special puzzle games have earlier final entry times. Check the official ticket page and opening calendar before you go.

Mu Valley is inland in Iwakuni, Yamaguchi, rather than in central Hiroshima. We drove there during a longer road trip, and a car made the visit easier.

The park advises drivers to follow National Route 187 and avoid certain narrow alternate roads suggested by navigation apps. There are local train and bus connections, but those require careful timetable planning.

FAQ for first-time visitors

Can two adults enjoy it without children?

We certainly did. We became fully absorbed in solving the puzzles and exploring the underground setting.

Is the current puzzle attraction available in English?

I haven't confirmed whether the current puzzle materials are available in English. If you need English instructions, contact the park before booking instead of assuming language support.

Does it work on a rainy day?

The main adventure is underground, so it can be a good rainy-day option. Severe weather may still affect opening hours or outdoor activities.

How much time would you allow?

At least three hours if you're including lunch and gold panning. Four or five hours is safer if you want to explore everything without rushing.

Final thoughts: an underground adventure I would happily repeat

Two adults, a hard puzzle course, no phone signal, mysterious underground scenery, and a little piece of gold on the first try.

I went in expecting to see an interesting cave. I came out feeling like I'd finished a small adventure.

If you enjoy puzzles, fantasy settings or unexpected detours around western Japan, Mu Valley is worth considering.
