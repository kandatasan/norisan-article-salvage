What kind of driving licence does Japan accept?

Japan recognises three main ways for a visitor or resident to drive: a valid Japanese driving licence, a qualifying 1949 Geneva Convention International Driving Permit (IDP), or a foreign licence from one of six recognised regions with an authorised Japanese translation.

Those six are Switzerland, Germany, France, Belgium, Monaco and Taiwan. A Japanese translation attached to a licence from a different country does not automatically give you permission to drive. An IDP under the 1968 Vienna Convention is not accepted by Japan.

Before you book a rental car

  • Confirm the original licence and any permit are valid and cover the vehicle you intend to drive.
  • For IDPs, verify that the issuing authority and the actual paper format comply with the 1949 Geneva Convention.
  • For an eligible translated licence, use an authorised Japanese translation and carry the original licence.
  • Check the one-year period from landing in Japan and the shorter document expiry date. Special counting rules can apply if you live in Japan and return after a short trip overseas.
  • Check the rental company's documents, age, credit card and insurance requirements before paying.

What if I cannot drive in Japan?

You can still have a wonderful trip! Central Hiroshima is easy to explore with trams and walking. Many destinations around western Japan are connected by train, bus or ferry. A car can give you more freedom for countryside detours, but it is only one way to travel.
