My wife heard that there was a hot spring town in Shimane where you could fill a little bottle with mineral-rich onsen water and take it home. A souvenir that's also spring water? That was unusual enough for us to take a drive.

So we went back to Tamatsukuri Onsen, an old hot spring district near Matsue. We've been here before, and it's always had the same gentle feeling: a river, small shops, places to wander and no real pressure to hurry.

We managed to get the hot spring bottle. We bought matcha latte. We went looking for an extraordinary dried persimmon I'd eaten years ago. And then I dipped my feet into one of the free outdoor footbaths.

It was HOT. Ridiculously hot.

Here's what happened, plus where the free footbaths and bottle-filling spot are if you want to try the same town.

Travel note: The personal scenes and photographs come from my own visits. Current town information, footbath locations and bottle rules were checked with the official Tamatsukuri Onsen tourism website in November 2026.

Are the Tamatsukuri Onsen footbaths really free?

Yes. The official tourist information currently lists three free footbath spots: two beside the Tamayu River and one covered footbath at Himegami Square.

That's one of the things I like about this town. You don't have to book a hotel or pay for a full bath to get a little taste of the hot springs. Take a short walk, sit by the water and give your feet a rest.

Himegami Square is a useful option if it's raining or the sun is strong, because the seating area is covered. It's also worth bringing your own small towel, although the official site currently says a footbath towel can be bought here.

The hot spring is not always at the comfortable temperature you might expect, though. I learned that in a memorable way.

First, the mission: a take-home hot spring bottle

My wife's tip sent us to Yu Yakushi Square, where the natural spring water can be collected in a bottle. The site has a simple area for filling containers, and there are small souvenir spray bottles for visitors who didn't bring their own.

The current official guide lists the souvenir beauty hot spring bottle for ¥200. The water itself comes from the hot spring, not an ordinary tap.

We bought a bottle and filled it. Mission accomplished.

There's a practical catch: the local tourism association asks visitors to use the collected water within around five days. This isn't a shelf-stable cosmetic product for the bathroom cabinet. It's natural hot spring water to enjoy promptly.

The water at the filling area can be hot. Handle the ladle and bottle carefully, and follow the posted instructions.

You can check the official beauty-spot guide and the Yu Yakushi Square visitor information before going.

I went looking for a dried persimmon from years ago

With the little hot spring bottle sorted out, something from a much older visit came back to me.

Years ago I'd eaten a dried persimmon from a shop in Tamatsukuri. It was so good that it reminded me of chocolate. Seriously—dried persimmon, and somehow I was thinking about chocolate.

Naturally, I wanted it again.

We wandered over to where I remembered the shop. But it had gone. No dried persimmon, no reunion with the strange chocolate-like fruit I'd been thinking about all these years.

That's the risk of going back to a place you remember fondly. The town was still lovely, but my very specific snack mission had failed.

A matcha latte and a walk beside the river

We bought matcha latte and kept walking along the Tamayu River. No dramatic sightseeing plan. Just a drink, the water beside us and little shops along the way.

This is the sort of slow holiday I enjoy here. If all you do is walk down the river and stop at a footbath, it still feels like an outing.

Then came the footbath.

I put my feet in. HOT!

I'd found a place by the river where I could sit with the matcha latte and relax. Excellent, I thought. This was going to be peaceful.

I dipped a foot in.

HOT! HOT! HOT!

I looked around and noticed an indication suggesting the water was hotter nearer one side. Aha. So this section is for the experts.

Fine. I'll move a little farther along. Now I can relax.

I tried again.

STILL HOT!

For a moment I felt personally deceived by the entire footbath.

It was a beautiful setting and, once I adjusted, still enjoyable. But the temperature really was a surprise. The official footbath guidance says the hot spring water is used close to its natural state, so it can be particularly hot on some days.

Test the water slowly with your foot before lowering both legs in. Don't force yourself to stay in if it feels too hot. Children should be especially careful.

Where are the three footbaths?

Tamatsukuri Onsen is a compact riverside hot spring district in Matsue, Shimane Prefecture. The current official guide describes two open-air footbaths along the Tamayu River and one covered footbath at Himegami Square.

All three are listed as free. Water levels and weather can affect access to riverside spots, so check signs and the official visitor guide when you arrive.

Do you need a rental car for Tamatsukuri Onsen?

We arrived by car because we were out on a drive, but you don't need a car just to walk around the hot spring town. Once you're in the central riverside area, the bottle-filling spot, shops and footbaths are within a pleasant walking district.

Travelers without a car can use trains to the Tamatsukuri-Onsen area and arrange the local transfer to the onsen town. Check current bus or taxi connections; the railway station isn't the same place as the central riverside footbaths.

If you're already driving through Shimane, it's a relaxing stop alongside other places around Matsue. Otherwise, I'd plan the transport first and enjoy the neighborhood on foot.

What should you bring?

  • A small towel: the easiest thing to forget when you want to dry your feet.
  • Comfortable shoes: the river and shops are best enjoyed as a slow walk.
  • A bottle if you want spring water: an official souvenir bottle is available if you arrive empty-handed.
  • Some time to wander: the fun isn't a rushed checklist; it's finding small things along the way.
  • A little caution: the water can be genuinely hot, especially when first entering.

Would I come back?

Of course. We found the hot spring bottle. The dried persimmon was gone. The matcha latte was good. The footbath seemed determined to cook my feet.

And somehow, that made me like the town even more. Tamatsukuri has the kind of gentle atmosphere that can turn a simple walk into a holiday memory.
